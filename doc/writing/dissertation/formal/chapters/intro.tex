\section{Background}

\iffalse

Mantle melting—driven by decompression, heating, or water release—
is a crutial geophysical 
process in lithosphere evolution and plate tectonics.
Volcanism, for instance, arises from mantle melting and melt segregation, 
producing Earth’s crust and shaping its chemical evolution.
Comparable processes may also occur on other terrestrial planets.

However, prevailing estimates of upper mantle temperatures, together the 
inferred state of stress in the lithosphere,
indicate that the asthenosphere is often partially molten 
by~\cite{Schmeling2000}.  
%Although most of Earth’s mantle lies below its melting point, 
Partial melting takes place in the following three key tectonic settings.

\vspace*{-6pt}\begin{description}  
 \item[\ \ Mid-ocean ridges:] Upwelling of the convecting mantle beneath mid-ocean ridges leads to
   decompression melting and generates the oceanic crust.
 \item[\ \ Hot-spots:] Thermal anomalies induce melting beneath hot-spots and generate ocean islands
   and oceanic plateaus as well as flood basalt provinces on the continents.
 \item[\ \ Subduction zones:] The addition of fluids that lower the melting point induces
   flux-melting above the dehydrating down-going oceanic crust and generates the continental crust.
\end{description}\vspace*{-6pt}

The partial melting phenomenon is hard to simulate,
within the mantle where partial melting happens, the porosity vanishes in regions where there is no
melt, which leads to highly degenerate equations of two-phase flow. In this thesis, by fully utilizing
newly developed advanced numerical scheme, we expect to study the dynamics of the partially molten Earth as it evolves in time,

Our results exhibits evolution of porosity and phase transition possibly happen during the process of partial melting.

A thorough understanding of partial melting is fundamental to comprehending the dynamics of mantle convection.
However, modeling partial melting in the mantle poses significant challenges, as 
porosity vanishes in melt-free regions, rendering the governing two-phase flow 
equations 
highly degenerate. In this thesis, we develop and apply advanced numerical 
schemes capable of robustly handling such degeneracies to study the temporal 
dynamics 
of a partially molten mantle. The simulations resolve the coupled evolution of porosity and phase transitions, providing a consistent framework for analyzing 
the thermomechanical processes associated with partial melting.

\section{Notable Related Work}

We review some existing software packages that address related aspects of the 
problem,
but differ in scope and methodology from our approach.

ASPECT : One of the most notable and advanced solver with excellent mesh 
adaptivity and numerics, and 
can simulate partial melting phenomenon.
It follows the discussion in~\cite{SchubertTurcotteOlson2001} and solves a Stokes system describing the movement of solid mantle 
material coupled with a advection-diffusion-reaction
equation system transporting energy and chemical species.
Phase change is included as a right hand side of the transport equation as a part of the reaction term.
``However, this will not consider melt extraction or any relative movement between melt and solid 
and there might be problems where the transport of melt is of interest".\cite{Bangerth2018}, which, on the contrary,
it is the main focus of our solver.

CitcomS : \cite{temdepenvis}, \cite{geoframework} A classical code used in
simulation of mantle convection. It solves an imcompressible Stokes problem 
coupled with energy transport, in consequence, melt extraction and chemical
species transport are not well represented.

TerraFERMA : \cite{TerraFerma} A high level wrapper for FEniCS,  PETSc and a science neural options handling system SPuD. The main
focus of the code is to provide a rapid and reproducible construction of multi-physics model arising in geophysics 
scenarios.

pMELTS/ MELTS: The MELTS software is designed to invectigate phase relations in magmatic systems during 
melting and crystallization.
However, mechanical behaviors and transport not included.
