In this chapter, we couple the locally mass-conserving MFEM and FVM methods
to perform numerical simulations of a partially molten mantle, with the remaining uncertainty in 
porosity $\phi$ addressed through phase package evaluation.
We describe the implementation details and coupling strategy that integrate the MFEM,
FVM, and the eutectic phase package. Before tackling the fully coupled problem, we first examine an alternative
porosity evolution scenario based on a prescribed melting rate. We then 
present an example that combines the MFEM, FVM, and eutectic phase package
into a fully coupled computation. The discussion restricts to pseudo-1D
scenarios and subsequently extends to full 2D simulations in the future work.

\section{Coupled Algorithm}
\label{sec:coupledalgo}
Let $\mathcal{U}^n=(C^n, H^n)$ denote the cell-averaged solution to the transport system~\eqref{dimensionlesstransport} at time $t^n$. 
Similarly, let $\mathcal{V}^n=(\check{\mathbf{v}}_{r}^n,\check{\mathbf{v}}_{s}^n,\check{q}_{l}^n,\check{q}_s^n)$ 
represent the solution to the Darcy-Stokes system at the same time $t^n$. Finally, let 
$\mathcal{W}^n=(\phi^n, \phi_1^n, \check{T}^n, c_{s}^n, c_{l}^n)$ 
denote the output of the phase calculation at $t^n$ obtained from the transport result 
$\mathcal{U}^n$. 

For each time step the time $t^n$, we begin by reconstructing the dimensionless mass composition
and enthalpy from the cell-averaged solution $\mathcal{U}^n$ using ML-WENO reconstruction at each data point of interest. 
The eutectic phase package then provides the corresponding phase parameters $\mathcal{W}^n$ 
based on these point wise values. With $\mathcal{W}^n$ determinedin particular with $\phi^n$, we solve the Darcy-Stokes
system at the fixed time $t^n$ to obtain $\mathcal{V}^n$. Finally, the
cell-averaged solution $\mathcal{U}^{n+1}$ at the next time 
$t^{n+1}$ is then obtained by forward marching of the transport system.
The complete procedure is summarized as a coupled solver in Algorithm~\ref{alg:coupled}.

\subsection{Discontinuous Porosity}
\label{sec:disporo}
Due to the presence of no melt regions and distinct eutectic phase regions, 
the reconstructed values of the dimensionless enthalpy and mass concentration may 
differ on the two sides of an edge shared by adjacent elements.
This, in turn, can lead to two different porosity values being computed for the same edge.
%However, it is crucial to have one compatible and appropriate porosity value used 
%on the element edge in the conforming MFEM scheme. 
However, in a conforming MFEM scheme, it is essential to assign a single consistent 
and physically appropriate porosity value on each element edge.

Therefore, we evaluate the harmonic average of the two values of porosity 
$\phi_e^+$ and $\phi_e^-$ at each quadrature points on both sides 
of an element edge $e$ to obtain
\begin{equation}
		  \overline{\phi}_e = \frac{2\phi_e^{+}\phi_e^{-}}{\phi_e^{+} + \phi_e^{-}}.
\end{equation}
%where $\phi_e^{+}$ and $\phi_e^{-}$ are porosity evaluated from two 
%sides of the element edge $e$.
The harmonically averaged porosity $\overline{\phi}_e$ is then used in
the continuous compaction equation~\eqref{eq:weak_continuous}, 
where the face integral is converted to an edge integral via the divergence theorem.

%When the porosity on one side equal zero, the geometric average of the porosity equals zero as well, which
%highlights the fact that there is no liquid flow should travel through this edge.
Importantly, when the porosity on one side of the edge vanishes, 
the geometric average also becomes zero. This property enforces the physical condition 
that no liquid can pass directly through an edge adjacent to a no-melt region.

\section{Pseudo One-Dimensional Column Tests}
\label{sec:pseudo1d}
In this section, we test the coupled algorithm on a two-dimensional $M\times N$ grid with a pseudo one-dimensional 
compaction column problem.
We study a compacting column aligned along the $y$-direction, defined over the domain $y\in[-H,0]$ and
$x\in [-L,L]$.
Two scenarios are investigated.
\begin{itemize}
\item Prescribed melting case: The MFEM and FVM solvers are coupled without phase computation.
Instead, a simple 
prescribed melting function $\Gamma(\mathbf{x})$ is used.
\item Phase calculation case: Phase transition and porosity evolution are computed simultaneously, 
providing a complete coupling between the MFEM, FVM, and eutectic phase package. 
\end{itemize}

For the two tests presented below, we fix the domain dimension in $x$-direction $L = 0.1$.
We fix mesh resolution in the $x$-direction as $M=2$ and pick different resolutions in $y$-direction.
The domain dimension and mesh resolution in $y$-direction are chosen accordingly.
We fix the parameter $\Theta = 0$ as well. 
The code is implemented in PETSc/C++, with implementation details given in the appendix.

\subsection{Prescribed Melting Cases}
\label{subsec:prescribed}
Before coupling the mechanical, transport and phase packages together into 
one integrated computation, 
we first illustrate a simpler model that couples MFEM and FVM
solvers without involving the phase package.
In this reduced setting, the porosity distribution is not determined by phase-splitting calculations. 
Instead, we prescribe the melting rate or, equivalently, the porosity evolution through a fixed source function distributed in space as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gamma}
\phi(t) = \Gamma(\mathbf{x}).
\end{equation}
We express conservation of liquid mass by introducing the prescribed 
melting term $\Gamma(\mathbf{x})$ as a source term on the right-hand
side of the advection equation as
\begin{equation}
		  \frac{\partial \phi}{\partial t} + \nabla \cdot (\phi \mathbf{v}_l) = \Gamma,
\end{equation}
where the diffusion term is being dropped for simplicity in this case.

%We perform a quasi-1D column test on a $2\times40$ rectangular physical domain $\Omega \in [-0.05,0.05] \times [-0.4,0]$.
We prescribe the following boundary conditions for the MFEM and the FVM solvers respectively to replicate the behavior of the one-dimensional
problem within the two-dimensional grid.

						  \end{equation*}

										  \begin{equation*}

In this section, we test the MFEM and FVM solvers combined with the eutectic phase package, which gives the 
fully integrated simulation framework. In this setting, porosity is no longer prescribed through an externally 
imposed melting function but is instead computed consistently from the transported nondimensional bulk composition and enthalpy. 

The computational domain represents an upwelling mantle column, with corner flow neglected near the surface under the 
simplifying assumption that the column continues its vertical ascent through the lithosphere. This configuration constitutes 
a minimal yet dynamically representative test case for assessing the stability and accuracy of the coupled algorithm in the 
presence of phase transitions and melt–solid interactions.

We propose some simplifications for our simulations.
First of all, we reconsider the transport equations~\eqref{dimensionlesstransport} and 
the simplified version is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:dlessadvecnodiff}
		  \begin{aligned}
					 \frac{\partial C}{\partial \check{t}} + \nabla \cdot \check{\mathbf{v}}_{\text{eff}} C &= 0,\\
					 \frac{\partial H}{\partial \check{t}} + \nabla \cdot (\check{\overline{\mathbf{v}}} \check{T}) - 
					 \check{L} \nabla \cdot ((1-\phi) \check{\mathbf{v}}_s) -  
					 \frac{\alpha_\rho l_0}{c_p} \check{T} \check{\overline{\mathbf{v}}} \cdot \mathbf{g} &= 0.
		  \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
\iffalse
%where the diffusion terms in both mass composition and enthalpy are dropped.
We drop the diffusion term  in liquid phase due to the porosity being small and relatively small diffusivity coefficient.
We also drop the diffusion term $\frac{\kappa}{u_0l_0} \nabla^2 \check{T}$
in energy transport equation for the fact that we are assigning constant or linear initial
conditions.
\fi
In this formulation, the diffusive contributions in both the mass composition and enthalpy equations are neglected.
Specifically, the diffusion term $\frac{\phi \mathcal{D}_l}{u_0l_0} \nabla c_l$ in the liquid phase is omitted
due to the fact that the porosity $\phi$ remains small ($\phi \ll 1$) and the associated diffusivity coefficient $\mathcal{D}_l$

\subsubsection{Compaction Length}
The compaction length is typically defined as
\begin{equation}
\delta_c = \sqrt{\frac{\kappa_\phi(\zeta + 4\eta/3)}{\mu_l}},
\end{equation}
where $\zeta$ and $\eta$ are bulk and shear viscosities of the solid mantle, respectively.
The compaction length an important intrinsic length scale that characterize the interaction between the
pressure difference and variations in fluid flux (see~\cite{MagmaPhysics} and~\cite{flowindeformableporousp1}).

In this work, we pick a simple constitutive relationship as shown in equation~\eqref{eq:bulkviscosity},
which is $\zeta = \mu_s/\phi$ in the notation of this section
and a power-law permeability as $\kappa_\phi = \kappa_0 \phi^2$.
Assuming that the shear viscosity $\eta$ is much smaller than the bulk viscosity $\zeta$, 
the appropriately scaled compaction length can be written in terms of the nondimensionalization length scale 
as
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:scaledcompactionl}
\delta_c = \sqrt{\frac{\kappa_0 \mu_s}{\mu_l} \phi} = l_0 \sqrt{\phi},
\end{equation}
where $l_0$ represents the characteristic length scale used in the nondimensionalization.
Thus $\delta_c = \delta(\phi)$ depends on the (evolving) porosity. 
In geological settings, a constant reference porosity is typically prescribed to define the compaction length. 
However, this assumption is not adopted in the present study, hence we use dimensionless length in the 
subsequent plots.

\subsubsection{Numerical Flux}
We modify the semi-discretized formulation of a general conservation law, as presented in equation~\eqref{eq:fvdis},
to incorporate a ``source" term $S$. The resulting expression can be written as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fvdis}
		  \frac{\partial \overline{u}_{E}}{\partial t} + \sum_{i=0}^4\frac{\lvert e_i \rvert}{\lvert E \rvert}
		  \sum_{g=0}^G \omega_{i,g} F(\mathcal{R}_{i,g}^+, \mathcal{R}_{i,g}^-)  
		  + \sum^H_{h=0}\xi_{h} S(\mathcal{R}_h) = 0,
\end{equation}
where $\omega_{i,g}$ represents the weight associated to the $g$-th quadrature point on edge $e_i$ and
$\xi_{h}$ represents the weight associated to the $h$-th quadrature point on the cell.
In practice, we employ the midpoint rule for the numerical integration over cells.
This allows us to approximate the face contributions using the cell-averaged transport variables while 
retaining second-order accuracy.

The adiabatic cooling term $\frac{\alpha_\rho l_0}{c_p} \check{T} \check{\overline{\mathbf{v}}} \cdot \mathbf{g}$ in equation~\eqref{eq:dlessadvecnodiff} is 
treated as a ``source" term, which is 
straightforward to implement within the semi-discretized formulation.
The corresponding Lax-Friedrichs numerical fluxes of the mass composition and the enthalpy across
element edges are expressed 
in terms of the ML-WENO reconstructed values $\mathcal{R}_C$ and $\mathcal{R}_H$ as
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:numflux}
\begin{aligned}
		  F_C(\mathcal{R}^+_C, \mathcal{R}^-_C) &= \frac{1}{2}\check{\mathbf{v}}_{\text{eff}}\cdot \pmb{\nu}
		  \Big[\mathcal{R}^+_C + \mathcal{R}^-_C - \alpha_C\big(\mathcal{R}^+_C - \mathcal{R}^-_C\big)\Big],\\
		  F_H(\mathcal{R}^+_H, \mathcal{R}^-_H) &= 
		  \frac{1}{2}\check{\overline{\mathbf{v}}}\cdot \pmb{\nu}
		  \Big[\check{T}^+ + \check{T}^- - \alpha_T \big(\check{T}^+ - \check{T}^-\big)  \Big] - \check{L} (1-\phi)\check{\mathbf{v}}_s\cdot\pmb{\nu},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $\pmb{\nu}$ is the ourward unit normal of the edge, and $\check{\mathbf{v}}_{\text{eff}}$ and $\check{\overline{\mathbf{v}}}$ 
denote the effective velocity and phase averaged velocity continuous across the edges.
The terms $\check{T}^+ = \check{T}(\mathcal{R}^+_H, \mathcal{R}^+_C)$ 
and $\check{T}^- = \check{T}(\mathcal{R}^+_H, \mathcal{R}^+_C)$ 
are temperatures evaluated on both sides of the edge using the eutectic
phase package, based on the reconstructed pointwise mass composition and enthalpy
. The stabilizers $\alpha_C$ and $\alpha_T$ use the local Lax-Friedrich scheme as shown in equation~\eqref{eq:loclf},
ensuring stability of the flux discretization.

The calculation of the numerical flux~\eqref{eq:numflux} of the mass composition is not 
entirely straightforward.
\iffalse
The primary transport variables of the mass composition and the enthalpy may be
reconstructed differently due to the presence of discontinuity and distinct phase regions,
which results in different values of porosity $\phi$ on both sides of the edges.
In the following simulations, we evaluate porosity on both sides as $\phi^+$ and $\phi^-$ and use
the harmonic average $\overline{\phi}$ on the edge as the compatible porosity as discussed in section~\ref{sec:disporo}.
\fi
The primary transport variables—mass composition and enthalpy—may undergo distinct reconstructions across an edge due 
to discontinuities and the presence of multiple phase regions. As a consequence,
the porosity $\phi$ can take different values, denoted $\phi^+ = \phi(\mathcal{R}^+_H, \mathcal{R}^-_C)$ and $\phi^- = \phi(\mathcal{R}^-_H,
\mathcal{R}^-_C)$.
To ensure compatibility in the MFEM discretization, we adopt the harmonic average of the two values, as discussed
previously in Section~\ref{sec:disporo}, and defined the compatible porosity on the edge as
\begin{equation*}
		  \overline{\phi}_e = \frac{2\phi_e^{+}\phi_e^{-}}{\phi_e^{+} + \phi_e^{-}}.
\end{equation*}
In contrast, the bulk mass composition represents a relatively smooth advected quantity. 
A harmonic average would add artificial damping and suppress mixing, while an arithmetic average
is more suitable without over-penalizing discontinuities. Therefore, we adopt
\begin{equation}
c^e = (c^+ + c^-)/2.
\end{equation}
We now revisit the definition of effective velocity defined in equation~\eqref{eq:definitioneffvel} 
with these interface definitions as
\begin{equation}
\bold{v}_{\text{eff}} = \frac{c^e_l \overline{\phi} \bold{v}_l + 
c^e_s(1-\overline{\phi}) \bold{v}_s}{c^e_l\overline{\phi} + c^e_s (1-\overline{\phi})},
\end{equation}
where $\overline{\phi}$ is the harmonic-averaged porosity and $c^e_s$, $c^e_l$ are the arithmetic averaged
mass compositions. 

Similarly, the phase averaged velocity required in the enthalpy flux is expressed without ambiguity as
\begin{equation}
\check{\overline{\mathbf{v}}} = \overline{\phi}\mathbf{v}_l  + (1-\overline{\phi})\mathbf{v}_s,
\end{equation}
where the same harmonic averaged porosity is employed.
This guarantees that in the limiting case $\phi^+ = 0$ or $\phi^- = 0$, the phase-averaged velocity reduces
to the solid velocity, thereby preserving the correct physical behavior and preventing artificial
transport of enthalpy associated with liquid in no-melt regions.

\section{Two-Dimensional Test}
\label{sec:twodexp}

two-dimensional $M\times N$
grid with a full two-dimensional problem.

outflow is permitted only through the top boundary, a constant 
upwelling velocity is imposed at the bottom boundary, and 
no-flow conditions are applied on the lateral boundaries.
The two-dimensional ML-WENO(3,2) stencils, illustrated in 
Figure~\ref{fig:quadmesh}, 
are employed without including the constant stencil.

composition and a similar initial condition of the bulk 
dimensionless enthalpy as
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}

&2.9 + 2.8\times 1.5, \quad &&-2\leq y < -1.5.
		  \end{alignedat}
		  \right.\\
		  C &= \left \{
					 \begin{alignedat}{2}
								&0.15,\quad && -0.2 \leq x < 0.2,\\
