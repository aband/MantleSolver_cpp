\section{Stokes Problem}
We briefly describe solving static Stokes equation with mixed finite element scheme.
We begin with strong form of the Stokes problem,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:strongStokes}
\begin{aligned}
-\Delta \bold{u} + \nabla p &= \bold{f}, \\
\nabla \cdot \bold{u} &= 0.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
We derive weak form 
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weakStokes}
\begin{aligned}
(\nabla \bold{u} , \nabla \bold{v})_{\Omega} - (p,\nabla \cdot \bold{v})_{\Omega} &= (\bold{f},\bold{v})_{\Omega} + 
\langle\tau^T \nabla \bold{u} \nu, \bold{v}\cdot \tau\rangle_{\Gamma_i},\\
&+ \langle\nu^T \nabla \bold{u} \nu - p_0, \bold{v}\cdot \nu\rangle_{\Gamma_i} - \langle\nabla \bold{u}_0, \nabla \bold{v}\rangle_{\Gamma_u}\\
(\nabla \cdot \bold{u}, \omega) &= 0.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Let stress $\bold{s}\cdot \tau = \tau^T \nabla \bold{u} \nu $ and  $\bold{s} \cdot \nu =  \nu^T \nabla \bold{u} \nu - p_0$, 
we have traction boundary condition term defined on $\Gamma_i$ which is $<\bold{s},\bold{v}>_{\Gamma_i}$.
We employ a test space $\{ \bold{v} \in H^1(\Omega) : \bold{v} = 0$ on $\Gamma_u \}$ here,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weakStokes2}
\begin{aligned}
(\nabla \bold{u}_h , \nabla \bold{v}_h)_{\Omega_h} - (p_h,\nabla \cdot \bold{v}_h)_{\Omega_h} &= (\bold{f},\bold{v}_h)_{\Omega_h} + 
		  \langle\bold{s}, \bold{v}_h\rangle_{\Gamma_{i,h}} - \langle\nabla \bold{u}_{0,h}, \nabla \bold{v}_h\rangle_{\Gamma_{u,h}},\\
(\nabla \cdot \bold{u}, \omega) &= 0.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
We can separate normal and tangent part of stress on the boundary easily when physical boundary align with x-y axis.
In the situation when a rectangular computational domain is used, we can mix stress and dirichlet boundary condition
even further.

We form a double saddle point system by symmetrically permutate original linear system,
\begin{equation}
\begin{bmatrix}
\bold{A}_s   & \bold{0}     & -\bold{B}_s &  \bold{0}   \\
\bold{0}     & \bold{A}_d   &  \bold{0}   & -\bold{B}_d \\
\bold{B}_s^T & \bold{0}     &  \bold{C}_s &  \bold{K}   \\
\bold{0}     & \bold{B}_d^T &  \bold{K}   &  \bold{C}_d
\end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix}
\bold{x}_s\\
\bold{x}_d\\
\bold{y}_s\\
\bold{y}_d
\end{bmatrix}= 
\begin{bmatrix}
\bold{f}_s\\
\bold{f}_d\\
\bold{g}_s\\
\bold{g}_d
\end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation}
We group these block matrix to form a concise version,
\begin{equation}
\bold{A} = \begin{bmatrix}
\bold{A}_s   & \bold{0}    \\
\bold{0}     & \bold{A}_d  
\end{bmatrix},
\quad
\bold{B} = \begin{bmatrix}
-\bold{B}_s & \bold{0}   \\
\bold{0}   & -\bold{B}_d
\end{bmatrix},
\quad
\bold{C} = \begin{bmatrix}
- \bold{C}_s &  -\bold{K}   \\
- \bold{K}   &  -\bold{C}_d
\end{bmatrix},
\quad \bold{F} = \begin{bmatrix}
\bold{f}_s \\
\bold{f}_d
\end{bmatrix},
\quad
\bold{G} = \begin{bmatrix}
-\bold{g}_s \\
-\bold{g}_d
\end{bmatrix},
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\begin{bmatrix}
		  \bold{A}   & \bold{B} \\
		  \bold{B}^T & \bold{C}
\end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix}
\bold{x} \\ \bold{y}
\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}
		  \bold{F} \\
		  \bold{G}
\end{bmatrix},
\end{equation}
where $\bold{A}$ is a positive definite matrix. However,
$\bold{B}$ is usually a singular matrix when no melting region presents.
We will present a modified Uzawa iteration algorithm to deal with this
coupled degenerate singular system.
Inexact Uzawa algorithm has been introduced in the previous literature 
\cite{InexactUzawa_Elam_Golub} and \cite{AnalysisInexactUzawa_Bramble_Pasciak_Vassilev},
\begin{algorithm}
\caption{Preconditioned Inexact Uzawa Iteration}
		  \begin{algorithmic}[1]
					 \STATE Initialize $\bold{x}_0 = \bold{0}$ and $\bold{y}_0 = \bold{0}$
					 \STATE Update $\bold{x}_{i+1} = \bold{x}_i + \bold{A}^{-1} (\bold{F} - (\bold{A}\bold{x}_i + \bold{B}\bold{y}_i))$
					 \STATE Obtain $\bold{r} = \bold{B}^T\bold{x}_{i+1} + \bold{C}\bold{y}_{i} - \bold{G}$
					 \STATE Obtain $\tilde{\bold{y}} =\text{argmin}_{\bold{x}\in V} ||(\bold{B}^T\bold{A}^{-1}\bold{B} - \bold{C})^{-1}\tilde{\bold{y}}-\bold{r} ||$
					 \STATE Update $\bold{y}_{i+1} = \bold{y}_i + \tilde{\bold{y}}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}
