\subsection{Test Darcy Problem}
We test our BDM element on a simple darcy problem as
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\bold{u} + \nabla p & = \bold{g}\\
\nabla \cdot \bold{u} & = 0
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

weak form

\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
(\bold{u}, \bold{v})_{\Omega} - (p, \nabla \cdot \bold{v})_{\Omega} &= (\bold{g},\bold{v})_{\Omega} 
- \langle p_0, v_n\rangle_{\Gamma_i} - \langle\bold{u}_0, \bold{v}\rangle_{\Gamma_u}\\
(\nabla \cdot \bold{u}, \omega) &= 0
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

Test space $\{\bold{v} \in H(\text{div},\Omega): v_n =0$ on $\Gamma_u\}$.

\subsection{Test Stokes Problem}
We test our modified BR element on a test Stokes problem. 
We begin with the strong form of the static Stokes problem as 
\begin{equation}\label{eq:strongStokes}
\begin{aligned}
-\Delta \mathbf{u} + \nabla p &= \mathbf{f}, \\
\nabla \cdot \bold{u} &= 0,
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $\pmb{\tau}$ is the deviatoric stress similar to~\eqref{eq:deriatoricstress} as 
$\pmb{\tau}(\mathbf{u}) = 2\big(\mathcal{D} \mathbf{u} - \tfrac{1}{3}\nabla \cdot \mathbf{u} \mathbf{I}\big) $.
Let test function space
\begin{equation}\label{eq:testspace}
		  V = \{ \mathbf{v} \in (H^1(\Omega))^2 : \mathbf{v} = \mathbf{0} \quad \text{on} \quad \Gamma_u \}
\end{equation}
We derive weak form 
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weakStokes}
\begin{aligned}
(\nabla \bold{u} , \nabla \bold{v})_{\Omega} - (p,\nabla \cdot \bold{v})_{\Omega} &= (\bold{f},\bold{v})_{\Omega} + 
\langle\tau^T \nabla \bold{u} \nu, \bold{v}\cdot \tau\rangle_{\Gamma_i},\\
&+ \langle\nu^T \nabla \bold{u} \nu - p_0, \bold{v}\cdot \nu\rangle_{\Gamma_i} - \langle\nabla \bold{u}_0, \nabla \bold{v}\rangle_{\Gamma_u}\\
(\nabla \cdot \bold{u}, \omega) &= 0.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Let stress $\bold{s}\cdot \tau = \tau^T \nabla \bold{u} \nu $ and  $\bold{s} \cdot \nu =  \nu^T \nabla \bold{u} \nu - p_0$, 
we have stress boundary condition term defined on $\Gamma_i$ which is $<\bold{s},\bold{v}>_{\Gamma_i}$.
We employ a test space 
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weakStokes2}
\begin{aligned}
(\nabla \bold{u}_h , \nabla \bold{v}_h)_{\Omega_h} - (p_h,\nabla \cdot \bold{v}_h)_{\Omega_h} &= (\bold{f},\bold{v}_h)_{\Omega_h} + 
		  \langle\bold{s}, \bold{v}_h\rangle_{\Gamma_{i,h}} - \langle\nabla \bold{u}_{0,h}, \nabla \bold{v}_h\rangle_{\Gamma_{u,h}},\\
(\nabla \cdot \bold{u}, \omega) &= 0.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
We can separate normal and tangent part of stress on the boundary easily when physical boundary align with x-y axis.
In the situation when a rectangular computational domain is used, we can mix stress and dirichlet boundary condition
even further.
