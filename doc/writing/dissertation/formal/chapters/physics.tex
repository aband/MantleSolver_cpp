\section{Multiphase conservation laws}
Fluid dynamics of the mixture of two phase flow has been extensively discussed.
I will disscuss the three governing equations of mass, momentum and energy referring
to discussion in \cite{MagmaPhysics} and \cite{DynamicsPartiallyMoltenRock}.
I begin with the previous discussion on two-phase flow.
Considering a conservation equation for given volumetric variable $\gamma$ in 
a representative volume element $V$.
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conservationlaw}
		  \frac{d}{dt} \int_{V} \gamma d\bold{v} = 
		  - \int_{\partial V} \bold{f} \cdot d\sigma + \int_{V} \bold{S} d\bold{v}
\end{equation}
We also define the quantity of porosity rigorously with the help of an 
indicator function admitting that multiple phase cannot coexist at the same
spatial point, which are regarded as immiscible phases.
We define indicator function:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:indicatorfunction}
		  i(\bold{x}) = \left \{ 
		  \begin{aligned}
					 &1 \quad \text{phase}_i\\
					 &0 \quad \text{not phase}_i
		  \end{aligned}
		  \right.
\end{equation}
We then define point wise macroscopic porosity function:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:porosityfunction}
		  \phi_i = \lim_{\delta \bold{v} \to 0} \frac{1}{\delta \bold{v}} 
		  \int_{\delta \bold{v}} i(\bold{x}) d\bold{v}
\end{equation}
