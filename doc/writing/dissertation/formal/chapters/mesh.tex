\section{Quadrilateral Mesh}
We consider a bounded computational domain $\Omega \subset \mathbb{R}^2$, where 
$\partial \Omega$ denotes the 
Lipschitz-continuous boundary of the domain.
For a given $h>0$, let $\mathcal{T}_h$ be a \textit{triangulartion} of the closure
of the domain $\overline{\Omega}$ made of quadrilaterals $E$, i.e.,
\begin{equation}
		  \overline{\Omega} = \cup_{E\in\mathcal{T}_h} E, \quad h_E < h,
\end{equation}
where two quadrilaterals are either disjoint or sharing one edge or one vertex.
We assume that the quadrilateral mesh is uniformally shape regular. 
A formal definition of uniform shape regularity of a 
quadrilateral mesh is given in~\cite{FinElemMethNavierStokes}.
\begin{defn}\label{def:shaperegularity}
For any quadrilateral $E \in \mathcal{T}_h$, we can get a set of four triangles
$T_{i}, \quad i \in \{0,1,2,3\}$. We form the triangle $T_i$ by excluding 
vertex $v_i$ from the quadrilaterl $E$, and use the remaining three vertex.
We define the following parameters as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:shaperegularity}
\begin{aligned}
		  h_E &= \text{diameter of E},\\
\rho_E &= 2 \min_{1\leq i\leq4} \{ \text{diameter of largest circle inscribed in} \hspace{0.2cm} T_i  \}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
A mesh partion $\mathcal{T}_h$ is considered to be uniformly shape regular 
if there exists a shape regularity parameter $\sigma_*$
independent of $\mathcal{T}_h$,
such that for all quadrilaterals $E \in \mathcal{T}_h$ we have the the ratio lower bound as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:regularityineq}
\rho_E/h_E \geq \sigma_* >0,
\end{equation}
where no subtriangle $T_i$ of $E$ has zero measure, i.e., each quadrilateral $E$ is assumed
to be strictly convex, not degenerate into triangle, line or point.
		  \end{defn}

The quadrilateral $E \in \mathcal{T}_h$ is referred to differently depending on the contexts.
In finite element method, a quadrilateral $E$ is being called as an \textit{element}, while
in finite volume method, it is typically referred to as a \textit{cell}.
For simplicity, we use the term \textit{element} throughout this section about mesh.

In Fiugre~\ref{fig:meshexample}, we show a reference element  
$\hat{E} = [-1,1]^2$,
a local reference element $\tilde{E}$
and a general quadrilateral element $E$.
The edges of element $E$ are denoted by $e_i$, $i\in\{0,1,2,3\}$, and the vertices of the 
element $E$ are denoted as $v_i$, $i\in \{0,1,2,3\}$ both numbered in the counterclockwise order.
We begin counting from zero, first to align with the indexing convention of C++, 
and second to facilitate representation in terms of modulo arithmetic, which
also starts from zero.
The vertices can be understood by $v_i = e_i \cap e_{i+1}$ 
, where $i\equiv i\Mod{4}$.
Here, $\pmb{\nu}_i$ and $\pmb{\tau}_i$ are used to represent the unit 
normal and the unit tangent
vector respectively. The unit normal vector $\pmb{\nu}_i$ points outerwards 
from the element $E$. The corresponding unit tangent $\pmb{\tau}_i$ is obtained by rotating 
$\pmb{\nu}_i$ counterclockwise, following the right-hand rule.

We assume the general quadrilateral element $E$ can be obtained by scaling from the local reference element $\tilde{E}$ such
that $\tilde{E} = E/H$, where $H$ is the maximal edge length of $E$, i.e. , the length of edge $e_1$ in Figure~\ref{fig:meshexample}.
Here, $\lvert e_1 \rvert = H$ and $\lvert \tilde{e}_1 \rvert = 1$.
There exists a non-affine bijective and smooth map $\mathbf{F}_{\hat{E}}: \hat{E} \rightarrow \tilde{E}$ mapping the vertices of $\hat{E}$ to
the vertices of $\tilde{E}$.
Note that the unit normal and the unit tangent vectors are invariant under scaling mapping $H$.

\section{Mathematical Preminaries}
In this section, we introduce some mathematical concepts that are relied upon in the
remainder sections. The theory and notations are presented in a general dimension
sense, but in practical, our discussion are confined to two dimension cases.
In addition, for simplicity,
we restrict our consideration to real-valued functions. 

To begin with, we recall the definition of a Sobolev space.
\begin{defn}\label{def:sobolevspace}
Let $\Omega \subset \mathbb{R}^d$ be a domain, $1\leq p \leq \infty$, and 
$m \geq 0$ be an integer. The Sobolev space of $m$ derivatives in $L^p(\Omega)$ is
\begin{equation}
W^m_p(\Omega) = \{f \in L^p(\Omega): D^\alpha f \in L^p(\Omega) \text{ for all multi-indices $\alpha$ such that } \lvert \alpha \rvert \leq m \},
\end{equation}
where the multi-indices $\alpha = (\alpha_0, \alpha_1, \ldots, \alpha_{N-1}) \in \mathbb{N}^N$ and
\begin{equation*}
\lvert \alpha \rvert = \sum_{i=0}^{N-1} \alpha_i.
\end{equation*}
The multi-indices derivative is defined as
\begin{equation*}
D^\alpha = \frac{\partial^{\lvert \alpha \rvert} }{ \partial x_0^{\alpha_0} \ldots \partial x_{N-1}^{\alpha_{N-1}} }.
\end{equation*}
For $f \in L^p(\Omega)$, the $L^p(\Omega)$ norm is
\begin{equation}
		  \norm{f}_{L^p(\Omega)} =\bigg( \int_{\Omega} \lvert f \rvert^p \bigg)^{1/p}.
\end{equation}
\end{defn}

We proceed with the definition of the Sobolev norm and seminorm.
\begin{defn}\label{def:sobolevnorm}
For $f \in W^m_p(\Omega)$, the norm $\norm{\cdot}_{W^m_p}$ is
\begin{equation}
\norm{f}_{W^m_p(\Omega)} = \bigg\{ \sum_{\lvert \alpha \rvert \leq m} \norm{D^\alpha f}^p_{L^p(\Omega)} \bigg\}^{1/p}, \quad p < \infty,
\end{equation}
and 
\begin{equation}
\norm{f}_{W^m_\infty(\Omega)} = \max_{\lvert \alpha \rvert \leq m} \norm{D^\alpha f}_{L^\infty(\Omega)} , \quad p = \infty.
\end{equation}
The seminorm $\lvert \cdot \rvert_{W^m_p}$ is
\begin{equation}
\lvert f \rvert_{W^m_p(\Omega)} = \bigg\{ \sum_{\lvert \alpha \rvert = m} \norm{D^\alpha f}^p_{L^p(\Omega)} \bigg\}^{1/p}, \quad p < \infty.
\end{equation}
\end{defn}
In the special case when $p=2$, we denote $W^m_2(\Omega) = H^m(\Omega)$. The corresponding norm and seminorn are then written as
$\norm{\cdot}_{H^m(\Omega)}$ and $\lvert \cdot \rvert_{H^m(\Omega)}$ respectively.
Recall the Trace Theorem in $H^m(\mathbb{R}^d)$ space.
\begin{thm}\label{thm:tracethm}
(Trace Theorem)

Let k and d be integers with $0<k<d$. The restriction map R extends to a bounded linear map from $H^m(\mathbb{R}^d)$ onto
$H^{s-k/2}(\mathbb{R}^{d-k})$, provided that $s>k/2$.
\end{thm}
\begin{proof}
See~\cite{funcAna}.
\end{proof}

Let $\mathbb{P}_r(\omega)$ denote the space of polynomials of degree up to $r$
on $\omega \subset \mathbb{R}^d$. The dimension of $\mathbb{P}_r$ can be
calculated as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dimpolynomial}
		  \text{dim}\mathbb{P}_r(\mathbb{R}^d) = \begin{pmatrix}
r+d \\
d
		  \end{pmatrix} = \frac{(r+d)!}{r!d!}.
\end{equation}
We refer to the Bramble-Hilbert lemma for basic interpolation error estimation for polynomials of degree r.
\begin{lem}\label{lem:BHlemma}
(Bramble-Hilbert Argument)

Let $\Omega \in \mathbb{R}^d$ be a domain, and for function $f \in W^m_p(\Omega), \hspace{0.1cm} m \geq 0,\hspace{0.1cm}  1\leq p < \infty$. There exists a constant $C$
such that the inequality holds, i.e, that
\begin{equation}
\inf_{p\in \mathbb{P}_{k-1}(\Omega)} \norm{f-p}_{W^m_p(\Omega)} \leq C h^{k-m}\lvert f \rvert_{W^k_p(\Omega)}, \quad j=0,1,\ldots,k,
\end{equation}
where the constant $C$ has an upper bound due to shape regularity assumption.
\end{lem}
\begin{proof}
		  See~\cite{EstimationofLinearFunctionals} and~\cite{ddd3654d-28ed-3763-8d70-a82ea44a3d31}.
\end{proof}
