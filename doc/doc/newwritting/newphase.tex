\section{Eutectic phase behavior}
Mid-ocean ridge forms at the place where temperature is generally lower.
The binary eutectic phase behavior describes the chemical behavior of 
two immiscible crystals from a completely miscible solution.

On a typical eutectic phase behavior diagram, we can locate any phase situation
using temperature and composition information. We follows phase behaviors
details described in \cite{DownwardOxidantTransport}. We made some reasonable adjustments
for our mantle system.

There are few key elements that allow practical calculation of phase region and 
corresponding volumetric fractions.

\begin{itemize}
\item Eutectic melting mass fraction is only related to pressure.
		  Fluid formed by eutectic melting has a constant composition of opx 
		  denoted as $X_e(P)$.
\item Melting temperature related to pressure. Pressure release is one of the 
		  main reason why melting happens in mantle.
		  $T_1(P)$ and $T_2(P)$.
\item Eutectic melting is the primal way of melting. The temperature where melting
		  happens has lower temperature then actual melting temperature.
\end{itemize}

We define following dimensionless bulk variables following \cite{DownwardOxidantTransport}.
We denote volumetric fraction of three phases as olivine $\phi_1$, opx $\phi_2$ and melt $\phi_f$.
We are tracking opx in the mixture for simulation.
Stefan number is defined as the ratio of the sensible heat to latent heat, $\text{Ste} = \frac{c_p \Delta T}{L}$.
We define dimensionless temperature with eutectic temperature as $\mathcal{T} = \frac{T - T_e}{T_{\text{MAX} - T_e}}$,
and dimensionless pressure as $\mathcal{P} = \frac{P-P_0}{P_0}$, where $P_0$ represents standard atmospheric 
pressure. We use melting temperature of olivine at standard atmospheric pressure as 2053 K, and 
1753 K for orthopyroxene (opx). 
Olivine has density range of 3.2 to 4.3 $g/cm^3$ and orthopyroxene has similiar density ranging from 3.2 to 4 $g/cm^3$.
We define a dimensionless composition $\chi_{i} = \frac{X}{X_e}$ where $i \in \{1,2,f\}$ and bulk composition defined
as $\chi = \sum \phi_i \chi_i$. The eutectic melting composition is now normalized as $\chi_e = X_e/X_e = 1$.
\begin{equation}
		  \begin{aligned}
					 C &= \phi_{2} \rho_{2}   + \phi_{f} \rho_{f} \chi_f(\mathcal{T}, \mathcal{P})\\
					 H &= \phi_{1} \rho_{1} c_{p,1} \mathcal{T} + 
							\phi_{2} \rho_{2} c_{p,2} \mathcal{T} +
							\phi_{f} \rho_{f} \big( L_{f} + c_{p,f}\mathcal{T} \big)
%					 H &= \phi_{1} \rho_{1} c_{p,1} \mathcal{T} + 
%							\phi_{2} \rho_{2} c_{p,2} \mathcal{T} +
%							\phi_{f} \rho_{f} \big( \frac{c_{p,1}}{\text{Ste}_1} (1-\chi_e)) + 
%                                             \frac{c_{p,2}}{\text{Ste}_2}\chi_e + c_{p,f}\mathcal{T} \big)
		  \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Where the melting latent heat can be affected by multiple things.
We can approximate the latent heat of the mixture as $$L_{f} = L_1(1-\chi_f(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{P})) + L_2\chi_f(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{P})$$.
We can nondimensionalize bulk composition and dimensionless bulk enthalpy as:
\begin{equation}
		  \begin{aligned}
					 \mathcal{C} &= C/\rho_1\\
					 \mathcal{H} &= H/(c_{p,1}\rho_1)
		  \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
We denote nondimensionalized density and specific heat as $\rho_{i,j}$ and $c_{p,i,j}$.

\subsection{Primary phase split}
In mantle, we mostly have olivine and orthopyroxene (opx) mixture in the rock.
Like eutectic melting system e.g. salt and ice,
the presence of one phase would largely
lower the melting temperature of another phase
to the eutectic melting point. We now decompose different phase regions in details
We are tracking bulk concentration of orthopyroxene (opx) in our calculation.
We identify following $6$ regions with different phase assemblages:
\begin{enumerate}[I]
\item Single-phase sub-solidus : olivine
\item Two-phase sub-solidus: olivine + opx
\item Three-phase eutectic: olivine + opx + melt
\item Two-phase super-eutectic : olivine + melt
\item Two-phase super-eutectic : opx + melt
\item Single-phase super-liquidus : melt
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Region I}
Pure olivine.
The conditions to be in region I are 
\begin{equation}
		  \mathcal{C} = 0 \quad \mathcal{H} \leq 1
\end{equation}
Hence, the volumetric fraction and temperature can be calculated simply as:
\begin{equation}
\phi_{1} = 1, \quad \phi_{2} = \phi_f = 0 \quad \text{and} \quad \mathcal{T} = \mathcal{H}
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Region II}
Two-phase sub-solidus: olivine + opx.
In this phase region, temperature hasn't reached eutectic melting temperature.
\begin{equation}
\phi_{2} = \frac{\mathcal{C}}{\rho_{2,1}} \quad \phi_1 = 1-\phi_2 \quad \phi_f = 0 \quad \text{and} \quad \mathcal{T} = \frac{\mathcal{H}}{\phi_1 + \phi_2\rho_{2,1} c_{p,2,1}} < 0
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Region III}
In this three-phase eutectic region, all three phases are present.
The temperature is fixed at the eutectic point.
This is the exact eutectic point where solid phase opx hasn't been 
exausted.
In this phase region, temperature is exactly at the eutectic temperature,
\begin{equation}
\phi_f = \frac{\mathcal{H}}{L\rho_{f,1}} \quad \phi_2 = \frac{\mathcal{C} - \phi_f\rho_{f,1}\chi_e}{\rho_{2,1}} \quad \phi_1 = 1-\phi_f - \phi_2 \quad \text{and} \quad \mathcal{T} = 0
\end{equation}
In this region, density of melt is fixed as eutectic density $\rho_f = \rho_e$.

\subsubsection{Region IV}
In this region, melting starts at the point left to the eutectic melting point.
This is the melting happening in upper mantle.
In this phase region, opx has exausted in solid matrix and temperature keeps rising.
As more olivine melting, the density of melting changes.
\begin{equation}
\phi_2 = 0
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Region V}
In this region, melting starts at the point right to the eutectic melting point.
We are not investigate too much in this type of melting.

\subsubsection{Region VI}
In this region, only melting is present.
Hence the volume fraction is given:
\begin{equation}
\phi_{1} = \phi_{2} = 0, \quad \phi_{f} = 1
\end{equation}

\subsection{Further approximation and simplified phase split}
To further simplify my calculation of phase diagram, I propose following approximations:
\begin{itemize}
\item  Olivine has density range of 3.2 to 4.3 $g/cm^3$ and orthopyroxene has similiar density ranging from 3.2 to 4 $g/cm^3$.
		 Hence, we assume olivine and orthopyroxene share one same density.
\item  Heat capacity has a complex dependency on temperature, pressure and mixture. We assume the same 
		  specific heat capacity under circumstance of mantle melting.
\item  Ignore both thermal expansion and density change upon melting.
\item  Assume latent heat $L_1 = L_2 = L$.
\end{itemize}
Under such assumption, we can get a much simplified concentration and bulk enthalpy:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{C} &= \phi_2 + \phi_f \chi_f(\mathcal{T}, \mathcal{P}) \\
\mathcal{H} &= (\phi_1 + \phi_2 + \phi_f)\mathcal{T} + \phi_f L_f \\
            &= \mathcal{T}  + \phi_f L
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

More importantly, we need $\mathcal{H}-\mathcal{C}$ phase diagram.
Now we calculate the line $b$ separate region III (eutectic melting) and region IV (super-eutectic melting).
Along line $b$, all opx has been exhausted and eutectic melting has stopped, hence $\phi_2 = 0$. 
The resulting $\mathcal{C} = \phi_f\chi_f$. According to our assumption of homogenous density, at
the solidus region, we have the following relationship:
$\chi = \frac{\phi_2}{\phi_2 + \phi_1} = \frac{M_2}{M_1 + M_2}$. Therefore, when all opx exhausted $\phi_2 = 0$,
$\phi_f = X/X_e = \chi$. Along this line $b$, $\chi_f = \chi_e = 1$ is a constant for the fact that all the melting
are eutectic melting. Hence along line $b$, we have
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{C} = \chi
\end{equation}
Now let's turn to another line $c$ separating region IV (super-eutectic melting) and region VI (super-liquidius region).
On this line $c$, all solid has molten $\phi_f = 1$. The resulting $\mathcal{C} = \chi_f = \chi$.

\subsubsection{Region II}
Two-phase sub-solidus: olivine + opx.
The conditions to be in region II are:
\begin{equation}
\quad \mathcal{H} < a
\end{equation}
In this phase region, temperature hasn't reached eutectic melting temperature.
\begin{equation}
\phi_{2} = \mathcal{C} \quad \phi_1 = 1-\phi_2 \quad \phi_f = 0 \quad \text{and} \quad \mathcal{T} = \frac{\mathcal{H}}{\phi_1 + \phi_2} = \mathcal{H} < 0
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Region III}
In this three-phase eutectic region, all three phases are present.
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{C} 
\end{equation}
In this phase region, temperature is exactly at the eutectic temperature,
\begin{equation}
\phi_f = \frac{\mathcal{H}}{L} \quad \phi_2 = \mathcal{C} - \phi_f\chi(\mathcal{T}, \mathcal{P}) \quad \phi_1 = 1-\phi_f - \phi_2 \quad \text{and} \quad \mathcal{T} = 0
\end{equation}
Where $\chi(\mathcal{T}, \mathcal{P}) = \chi_e(\mathcal{T}, \mathcal{P})$, all melting is 
eutectic melting. Mass fraction is fixed as eutectic mass fraction.

\subsubsection{Region IV}
In this super-eutectic two-phase region, opx has been exhausted in eutectic melting.
The volumetric fractions can be determined as:
\begin{equation}
\phi_2 = 0 \quad \phi_f = \frac{\mathcal{C}}{\chi(\mathcal{T}, \mathcal{P})}
\end{equation}
The corresponding temperature should be determined by solving the following equation.
\begin{equation}
F(\mathcal{T}) = \mathcal{H} - \mathcal{T} - \frac{\mathcal{C}}{\chi(\mathcal{T}, \mathcal{P})} L = 0 
\end{equation}
Simple Newton method can be applied to solve this equation if we apply nonlinear relationship.
If we use simple linear relationship between mass fraction and temperature $\chi = \chi_e(1-\mathcal{T})$,
we can obtain a simple quadratic equation:
\begin{equation}
		  \begin{aligned}
					 (1-\mathcal{T})\mathcal{H} - (1-\mathcal{T})\mathcal{T} - \mathcal{C} L/\chi_e(\mathcal{P}) &= 0 \\
					 \mathcal{T}^2 - (\mathcal{H} + 1)\mathcal{T} + \mathcal{H} - \mathcal{C} L/\chi_e(\mathcal{P}) &= 0
		  \end{aligned}
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Region VI}
In this single phase  super-liquidus region, we can get volume fractions and temperature easily as:
\begin{equation}
		  \phi_1 = \phi_2 = 0 \quad \phi_f = 1 \quad \text{and} \quad \mathcal{T} = \mathcal{H} - L
\end{equation}

\subsection{Pressure and melting point}
Now I take pressure change into account.
In general, the higher the pressure, the higher the eutectic melting point.
